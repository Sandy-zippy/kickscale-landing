Summary
B2B sales automation discussion centered on implementing WhatsApp API and CRM integration, with cost concerns arising from the proposed recurring software fees.

Current B2B Sales Operations
The B2B tire sales process relies on custom software for quotation generation, applying fixed discounts, and managing very thin margins of 1% to 2%. Sales operations include Dealer Sales Representatives managing 15 to 16 dealers, focusing on daily order taking, estimating, and dispatch preparation.

Proposed AI Automation Flow
The proposed Phase 1 automation involves using the WhatsApp API to scrape inquiries, generate quotations via custom software, and integrate with a CRM for lead tracking. The goal is to automate order taking and estimating to free up sales staff for customer-centric issues like claims and complex problems.

Project Scope and Cost Review
The automation project, including WhatsApp API integration and CRM setup, is estimated to take 1 sprint for a one-time cost of 1.5 lakh rupees. The recurring monthly cost of 15,000 to 16,000 rupees raised cost concerns because it was higher than the company's current manpower costs.

Details
* B2B Lead Generation and Quotation Process: Teg Sethi described their B2B sales process for tires, where leads are generated either by the company from dealers or through references in the market. The moment a lead is received, they create a quotation using custom software, selecting the variant, applying a fixed discount percentage based on a chart, and inputting the quantity to generate an estimate. The custom software helps control manual errors, such as incorrect pricing or excessive discounting, which is critical because margins in the tire industry are very thin, operating around 1% to 2% (00:00:55).
* Internal Team Management and Sales Operations: The company has created a community for every Dealer Sales Representative (DSR) where they are supposed to add all their dealers and form groups (00:01:49). This structure helps track how many dealers each person is handling, with each DSR currently managing about 15 to 16 dealers across 16 groups. The daily routine for the sales team involves calling dealers, taking orders, preparing, and finalizing the estimates, and creating dispatches based on established routes (00:03:03).
* Dispatch Notification and Current Tracking: Once orders are finalized, the dispatches are shared in a separate dispatch group by 5:00 PM, after which dispatching begins (00:03:56). Bhargav Naidu's Presentation inquired about how the status of each dispatch is tracked, and Teg Sethi explained that it is assumed that the salesperson who initiates the dispatch is responsible for ensuring it is made, otherwise, they do not receive an incentive. Currently, the low volume of sales means they are not facing major issues with this system, but they are seeking solutions to scale beyond the existing level (00:04:51).
* AI Automation Expectations for Sales Staff: Teg Sethi expressed a desire for AI to automate the repetitive tasks of order taking and estimating to free up the sales staff. The expectation is that the process would be automatic, involving a WhatsApp message to the dealer, which, upon response, would allow the system to auto-calculate the estimates and basic calculations based on a predefined list. The goal is for the dispatch list to be ready by 5:00 PM without manual involvement from the sales team, allowing them to focus on customer-centric issues like claims or complex problems (00:05:39).
* Custom Order Discounting Strategy: When dealers ask for discounts, the company uses a "variation sale" strategy involving a discount chart based on product volume. High-volume products, such as 12-inch and 13-inch tires, are given a higher percentage discount, while low-moving items, such as 17-inch tires, receive a lower percentage discount (00:07:25). This method creates a "window" of flexibility, allowing the sales staff to manage customer-requested discounts by compensating for them using the higher margins available on low-volume items (00:08:05).
* Proposed AI Automation Flow via WhatsApp: Bhargav Naidu's Presentation outlined a proposed automated flow starting when vendor inquiries come through WhatsApp groups (00:08:53). The AI would pick up the information, process it through the custom software to generate a quotation with appropriate discounts, and send it back to the vendor for clarification. If the vendor requests a better discount, the AI can propose a better policy within set limits; upon receiving a confirmation, the AI would generate a Purchase Order (PO) and notify the dispatch team (00:09:29).
* Payment Options and Credit Limit Management: Payment is collected either in advance or upon delivery via bank transfer or cash (00:09:29). The system needs to provide customers with the option to select either cash or bank transfer when the quotation is sent. Furthermore, every dealer account has a set limit, and if an order exceeds that limit, the AI must ensure the order is handled appropriately, such as dividing a large order into two to avoid exceeding the credit limit (00:10:23).
* Sales Team Intervention Benchmark and Scaling: The automation should limit sales team involvement to escalations or customized options, such as when a dealer places a high-ticket value order, like one above three or four lakh rupees (00:11:03). Bhargav Naidu's Presentation suggested that this benchmark would make high-value clients feel more connected to the brand, and then proceeded to suggest another option for increasing brand value and automating the order-taking process, introducing the concept of a landing page (00:11:43).
* Landing Page Concept and Custom CRM Demonstration: Bhargav Naidu's Presentation showed an example of a landing page used for another client in the driver aggregation business, where a client can enter all their requirements, including their care plan choice and custom requirements (00:12:30). This data is sent to a custom CRM that tracks the lead through various stages, such as 'New Lead,' 'Contacted,' 'Qualified,' and 'Onboarded' (00:14:08). The CRM also provides a dashboard to reconcile data, showing the revenue involved at each stage and allowing the team to track progress and pending tasks (00:15:04).
* Challenges with Landing Page Implementation: Teg Sethi noted that the suggested landing page approach requires the dealer to fill in the information themselves, which presents a challenge due to their clientele not being very technically educated or willing to use new technology (00:16:00). Teg Sethi emphasized that the transition to technology must be gradual and ready for the people involved (00:16:43). Teg Sethi confirmed that automation is desirable to reduce the growing cost of manpower, with the intention of replacing the efforts of multiple people with one person managing the automated system (00:17:37).
* AI Automation using WhatsApp API and CRM Integration: Bhargav Naidu's Presentation affirmed that automation via WhatsApp is possible using an API and suggested that the flow should involve scraping data from WhatsApp groups and feeding it into the custom software (00:18:25). They proposed linking this to a CRM to clearly visualize the status of every lead, ensuring that when volume increases, the team is prepared. The system would also use the WhatsApp API to send automatic client notifications at every stage movement (e.g., order confirmed, packing, delivery) to address the current problem of salespeople forgetting to update clients (00:19:51).
* AI Role and Lead Allocation in CRM: Bhargav Naidu's Presentation clarified that in the CRM, different access levels would be provided for admin and users, with all leads consolidated in one place and clearly assigned to a person (00:22:24). Teg Sethi theorized that the AI agent would become a salesperson itself, potentially taking over the work of underperforming DSRs (00:23:12). Bhargav Naidu's Presentation confirmed that a separate user for the AI would be created in the CRM, and the automation process would begin when a lead is assigned to the AI (00:23:46).
* Updating AI Data and Inventory Check: Teg Sethi inquired about updating the discount schemes and prices for the AI, and Bhargav Naidu's Presentation confirmed that access would be given to a vector database so they can constantly update the data (00:24:35). However, checking inventory is a complication because the stock is not only at their location but also at the company's location, and the company will not provide access to their software (00:25:12). The current flow will need to be adjusted so that the AI creates the lead, but then triggers a notification to the salesperson to manually confirm stock availability before the AI proceeds with the next steps (00:25:55).
* AI for Pricing Strategy and Dealer Performance Mapping: Teg Sethi inquired whether the AI could help map their pricing strategy and dealer performances, such as identifying which dealers are more profitable based on past orders and supply restrictions (00:26:40). Bhargav Naidu's Presentation confirmed that the AI could generate dashboards with inbound/outbound sales and cash collection data, but for complex pricing strategies, the AI would require specific data and training (00:27:40). Teg Sethi further elaborated on the manual process of mapping dealers into categories (small, large, extra-large volume) for different tire brands and prioritizing small volume dealers for profit generation (00:28:37).
* AI Manipulation and Project Phasing: Teg Sethi questioned if the AI could handle manipulative interactions, such as strategically pushing more profitable products when a low-availability item is requested (00:29:16). Bhargav Naidu's Presentation responded that AI could be "very manipulative" but that this capability relies entirely on the vector database and training model, confirming it would be a "phase two" project due to its complexity (00:30:02) (00:32:33). The primary project will focus on the automations from WhatsApp to order processing via the CRM (00:30:47) (00:32:33).
* Automation Proposal and Costs: Bhargav Naidu's Presentation summarized the proposed Phase One project, which includes WhatsApp API integration, CRM integration with manual stock confirmation, client notifications, and client retention activities (00:33:17). The timeline for this end-to-end automation process is estimated to take one sprint (one month). The cost for this single sprint is 1.5 lakh rupees, which is a one-time charge for the completion of the task (00:34:00).
* Recurring Costs and Value Discussion: The recurring monthly cost for the entire system (including the custom CRM, WhatsApp API paid to Meta, and official email ID) is estimated to be around 15,000 to 16,000 rupees (00:34:58). Teg Sethi expressed concern that the recurring software cost is higher than their current manpower costs, stating that they would expect the software to reduce their costs, not equalize or increase them (00:36:31) (00:38:29). Bhargav Naidu's Presentation concluded the meeting and committed to sending a detailed proposal outlining the required steps and expected output (00:39:19).
